# Tessellate deck assumptions

## Source state

Only the company name and concept were supplied: Tessellate retrofits existing warehouse conveyor lines with vision-guided sortation. No customer, traction, market, pricing, financial, fundraising, team, product-specification, or performance evidence was provided.

## Material assumptions used in the deck

- **Company stage:** Treated as pre-traction. The traction slide explicitly reports that no verified pilots, revenue, or outcomes were supplied.
- **Product:** Depicted as a concept architecture comprising overhead vision, edge routing software, and compatible diversion hardware. Final hardware interfaces, safety design, throughput, accuracy, and integration scope remain undefined.
- **Go-to-market:** Assumes a land-and-expand motion beginning with one paid assessment and one conveyor line, then expansion within a site.
- **Business model:** Proposes one-time assessment and retrofit revenue plus recurring control-software and support revenue. No prices, margins, sales cycles, or contract terms are claimed.
- **Market model:** Uses an illustrative bottom-up case of 4,000 target sites × 3 eligible lines per site × $125,000 first-year value per line = $1.5 billion. These inputs are planning assumptions, not sourced market research.
- **Team:** No people or biographies were invented. The deck instead identifies four capability areas that must be demonstrated or recruited.
- **Fundraise:** Assumes a $3.5 million seed round for 18 months, allocated 45% product and engineering, 30% pilots, 15% go-to-market, and 10% operations.

## Impact and next action

The deck is suitable as a clearly labeled narrative prototype, not as a diligence-ready investor document. Replace assumptions with source-backed product specifications, founder biographies, pilot evidence, pipeline, pricing, unit economics, market research, financing terms, and milestone budgets before external use.
